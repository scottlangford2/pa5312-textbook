# Datasets for Evaluation Practice

We practice evaluation methods on **real Texas data** before you apply them to your own final-project program or policy. All analysis is done in Microsoft Excel (enable the **Data Analysis ToolPak**).

## Texas County Panel (2000–2024)
One row per county per presidential election — **254 counties × 7 elections**. Political outcomes (turnout, two-party vote share), plus demographic and socioeconomic characteristics (population, race/ethnicity, median household income, poverty, unemployment, education). Excellent for group comparisons, regression with controls, and quasi-experimental thinking about county-level policy.

- Shared with the PA 3311 course; see its [codebook](https://github.com/scottlangford2/pa3311-textbook/blob/main/data/CODEBOOK_county_panel.md) for variables and sources (MIT Election Lab, Census PEP/SAIPE, USDA ERS).

## Texas City Finance Panel (2013–2024)
One row per city per year — **1,180 cities**. Sales-tax revenue, taxable sales, and (in a 2022 cross-section) debt and property taxes. Useful for evaluating the fiscal effects of local policy changes and for pre/post and difference-in-differences exercises.

## Bring-Your-Own Program Data
For the final evaluation project you will choose a program or policy and locate appropriate data — a public agency's administrative records, a published evaluation dataset, or one of the Texas panels above. We will discuss sourcing and feasibility in the proposal stage.

> **A note on causal claims.** None of these datasets came from a randomized experiment. A central skill of this course is judging *how credibly* a given design and dataset can support a causal claim about a program's effect — and stating the limits honestly.
